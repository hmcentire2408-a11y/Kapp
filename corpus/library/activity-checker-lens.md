## 0. What the list is for

The list is not scored by itself. It feeds an **Extracurricular rating** and a **decision sheet**, and readers turn it into shorthand codes [P:559, P:714-720].

- **The rating scale [H:168-183]:**
  - 3 = "solid participation but without special distinction."
  - 2 = "strong secondary school contribution… class president, newspaper editor… possible local or regional recognition; major accomplishment(s) that have had an impact outside of the classroom."
  - 1 = "possible national-level achievement."
- **Family responsibilities and term-time work** are explicitly rateable, and "should be included with other e/c to boost the rating" [H:180-183].
- **The job of the list:** Move the reader from 3 to 2 (or 2 to 1) in about 60–90 seconds [HF, unverified], in a form that survives compression into a code.

## 1. The screens, in order

### S1: Accuracy (pass/fail)
- **Grades, titles, and years** must match the newest direct statement and any other verifiable source, such as rosters, the counselor, or recommenders.
  - List **years of participation, not years of leadership**. Mark title years inline, e.g. "Captain (12)" [HF].
  - If an entry claims a role in a year before you joined, it fails. Fix the entry before working on anything else.
- **Present tense for things in progress.** A funded charter must not read like a finished product. A reader skimming fast will assume it's built.
- Never inflate hours or titles. **Renaming a title is only allowed when it clarifies the same office** without upgrading it. Hoffman's "District Governor" → "State Governor" is a borderline case [HF]. If an outsider would call the new name a promotion, don't use it.

### S2: Participation → leadership → impact → recognition
"Membership ≠ leadership; leadership ≠ impact; prestige ≠ accomplishment" (`doctrine/evaluation-rubric.md:41`). For each entry, name the highest rung it *shows on the page*, not the one you know it reached.

### S3: The verb test (impact vs. effort)
Sort the verbs in each description:
- **Effort/intent verbs** (score low): led, helped, organized, proposed, worked on, gained, developed, learned, was responsible for.
- **Result verbs** (score high): passed, won, adopted, built, launched, grew, selected, elected, earned, recruited.

Every "proposed X" should either become "X was adopted" or be cut. Every skill claim ("developed leadership") should be cut, because it describes you instead of showing a change.

### S4: Qualitative impact (what non-numeric impact looks like)
Impact is **a change that exists because of you, stated as a checkable fact**. It takes five forms:
1. **Adoption:** something you proposed was implemented or kept.
2. **Creation:** a resource, process, or team exists that didn't before.
3. **Trust:** you were chosen, elected, or given authority by other people. Selectivity ratios belong here.
4. **Continuity:** something kept going because of you, or after you.
5. **Recognition:** outside judges rated you. Name who judged ("judged by practicing lawyers").

Don't overload entries with numbers [HF]. Use a number only when it is the clearest evidence of scale, and never pile three numbers into one line.

### S5: Title legibility
Readers look for titles, and generic titles are common. There are "tens of thousands of NHS presidents" [W, digest:103-104]. An unusual title (e.g. "Attorney General," "De Facto Lead") needs its meaning stated right next to it. A selectivity ratio does this best.

**Claimed 2026-27 change [HF, unverified]:**
- The role field is now conditional, and the Preview shows **Organization, Role**, with the role *last*.
- Hoffman's workaround: put the role in the org field (reported 50-character limit), answer "No" to the role question, and separate with a comma or pipe ("Role | Org").
- **Use it** for strong or unusual titles, where putting the title first changes what the reader takes in.
- **Don't use it** when the org and role are nearly the same, or when the title is informal. Putting "de facto" first spotlights a title nobody officially gave you.

### S6: Context and opportunity
- Activities are judged "relative to your peers" at your school [C:453] and against "the opportunities, or lack thereof" you had [P:718-720].
- [A] wants "engagement with what was actually available to you, without blending into what everyone around you does — and if you are doing the common thing, excelling at it" (digest:326-331).
- Ask of each entry: **is this the common thing at my school? If so, does the entry show excelling, not just doing?**
- Readers are also told to ask whether the school believes the story. "If no one in your school believes it, it doesn't matter" [C:447]. The counselor letter has to be able to back up the top entries.

### S7: Coherence
- Readers flag lists that look contrived: "so many non-profit organizations founded… that's a bit contrived"; "nothing consistent… did the student do service just to make up for lack of activities?" [P:721-724].
- Internships "mostly don't register" unless they tell a story that fits the rest of the file [W, digest:105-109].
- **Co-curricular vs. extracurricular** [C:681-687]: an activity that directly matches your intended major reads as part of your academic track, not as range outside it. Neither is bad, but know which one each entry is.
- **"Junior joiner"** [HF]: the red flag is many unselective memberships starting in 11th grade. Late-starting *selective* roles, competitions, or elected positions don't trigger it. A few activities should run 3–4 years.

### S8: Placement
- About **five entries per PDF page** [HF, unverified]. Put your most significant, on-narrative, academic-interest entries in slots 1–5, including at least one or two long-running ones. Put role-less memberships, hobbies, self-study, and off-narrative items in slots 6–10 [HF].
- Harvard pins the recruited sport to slot 1 [H:75]. Order is an instruction readers use.

### S9: Hours
- **Claimed:** the form now asks for time "during the busiest parts of the year," best guesses OK [HF, unverified]. Seasonal activities like build season or competition prep should be entered at peak, and totals don't need to add up.
- Volunteering: roughly 250+ hours reads as solid [W, digest:118], but that's one office's impression.

### S10: Overflow
- **Additional Information:** only for the one or two entries where 150 characters hides something important. Keep it to about four sentences, no resume, no repeating the list [HF].
- **Letters:** recommenders should *not* restate activities [A, digest:322-323]. Anything a letter has to corroborate should come from the recommender's own observation.
- **Honors vs. activities:** if the outcome is only in the Honors section, the activity entry reads as effort. A one-line reference in the activity entry closes the gap, as long as it doesn't duplicate the honor line.
- **"Plan to continue in college":** mostly ignored. Coaches or teams sometimes use it to contact admitted students [HF].
