# Conflicts Ledger

The point of this file: a rule the model already agrees with displaces nothing. "Evaluate holistically" reads as agreement and changes no output. Only a named contradiction forces a resolution.

Each entry states the default view, the corpus position, the source, and a confidence. Confidence is load-bearing — a `low` entry should lose to a well-sourced argument, and saying so here is what keeps the whole file from becoming dogma.

Consult this file whenever a specific claim about admissions is in play.

---

### C1 — National Merit Semifinalist

**Default view:** A meaningful honor. Worth a Common App honors slot.
**Corpus position:** At NCSSM the base rate is high enough that it reads as ordinary in this applicant population, and the school report transmits it independently, so the slot is redundant.
**Sources:** `school-context/ncssm-residential-profile-2025.pdf` (84 finalists, both campuses); `admissions-research/ao-account-digest.md`
**Confidence:** Medium. The base-rate argument is sound; "near-invisible from repetition" is single-practitioner.

### C2 — Prestige of the activity vs. evidence of the applicant

**Default view:** Selective programs and recognizable names carry weight roughly in proportion to their selectivity.
**Corpus position:** What a reader can do with an activity is what matters. A national-sounding credential with no visible artifact, no third-party corroboration, and no described role is weaker than an unglamorous activity with all three.
**Sources:** `admissions-research/harvard-reading-procedures-class-2023.md`; `admissions-research/holistic-review-pecan-pie-paper.md`
**Confidence:** High. Both a primary process document and empirical research.

### C3 — School context

**Default view:** Apply a general national standard for rigor and achievement.
**Corpus position:** Evaluate against NCSSM-Morganton's specific opportunity set and grading. An achievement that sounds impressive nationally may be the local median; one that sounds modest may be unavailable to almost anyone at that school.
**Source:** `school-context/ncssm-residential-profile-2025.pdf`
**Confidence:** High.

### C4 — Hardship

**Default view:** Adversity is a credit. Naming it improves the file.
**Corpus position:** Adversity is context that reframes the record. The evaluable content is the response — what it constrained, what was done anyway. Adversity narrated without agency does not read as strength.
**Source:** `admissions-research/harvard-reading-procedures-class-2023.md`
**Confidence:** Medium-high. The historical procedures support this; current universal practice is an inference.

### C5 — Essay quality

**Default view:** Better prose is a better essay. Polish, imagery, and structure are the axes.
**Corpus position:** An essay is evidence in a file, not a writing contest. The test is what a reader can say about the applicant afterward that they could not say before, and whether it survives being retold in one sentence at committee. A beautifully written essay with no payload is a weak essay.
**Sources:** `essays/essay-evaluator-lens.md`; `essays/hack-the-college-essay-2017.pdf`
**Confidence:** High.

### C6 — School-level outcome data

**Default view:** A school that sends N students to a given college each year implies a rough individual probability.
**Corpus position:** Base rates describe the cohort, not the applicant. They constrain expectations; they do not produce odds.
**Source:** `school-context/ncssm-outcomes-analysis-4yr.md`
**Confidence:** High.

### C7 — Practitioner accounts

**Default view:** A former admissions officer describing their process is authoritative about admissions.
**Corpus position:** They are authoritative about *their* office in *their* era. Treat as process texture and hypothesis generation, never as universal law, and never above a primary document that contradicts them.
**Sources:** `admissions-research/ao-account-digest.md`; `admissions-research/ao-counselor-candid-talk.md`
**Confidence:** High as a methodological rule.

### C8 — Historical Harvard reading procedures

**Default view:** This document describes how selective admissions works.
**Corpus position:** It describes how one office worked in one era. It is the best primary process evidence in this repo and still not current universal policy. Ratings language borrowed from it is a working framework, not an institutional formula.
**Source:** `admissions-research/harvard-reading-procedures-class-2023.md`
**Confidence:** High.

## Adding entries

An entry earns its place only if the two positions actually differ. If the "default view" is something the model would already say, you have written a principle, not a conflict, and it is a principle, not a conflict, and does not belong here.
