# "Making a Pecan Pie" — findings digest

**Full citation.** Poorna Talkad Sukumar, Ronald Metoyer, Shuai He. *Making a
Pecan Pie: Understanding and Supporting the Holistic Review Process in
Admissions.* Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, Vol. 2,
CSCW, Article 169 (November 2018). University of Notre Dame.

**Why this is a digest and not the paper.** The paper is under ACM copyright.
This repository is public, so it carries findings and analysis rather than the
full text. Cite it as *[P]*, and cite this file for the findings below rather
than quoting the paper verbatim.

**Method, for weighing the evidence.** Cognitive task analysis of reviewers at
**one** private university admissions office. Observed behaviour, not a national
sample — it outranks anecdote because it is systematic observation, and it does
not establish that any other office works the same way.

---

## The findings Kapp actually uses

**1. The decision sheet becomes the application.** Every application gets a
decision sheet recording key points, codes and ratings. The paper's central
observation is that reviewers came to treat *reviewing* as synonymous with
*filling out the decision sheet*, and discussed both their colleagues' reviews
and the committee's cases in its terms. The file a student submits is not what
gets argued over; a compressed summary of it is.

*Consequence for an applicant:* anything that does not survive compression into
a few lines effectively does not reach committee. A quality that needs three
paragraphs to establish will not be there.

**2. Detail is lost between reading and committee.** Reviewers' understanding at
the time of reading amounted to more than the brief summaries they entered. That
excess is not recoverable later — the static representations impose a working-
memory and cognitive-load cost, so by committee the sheet is what remains.

*Consequence:* time between reading and decision degrades everything not written
down. Memorability is not a bonus; it is the transmission mechanism.

**3. Reviewers are susceptible to known cognitive biases.** The authors identify
potential biases by comparing documented heuristics against reviewer tasks, and
note that even experienced reviewers are susceptible — expertise does not
inoculate. They are explicit that ranking which biases matter most is non-trivial,
so treat the list as identified-and-plausible, not measured-by-effect-size.

*Consequence:* order of reading matters. Academics are encountered first and
later material tends to be read for confirmation of the impression already
formed, so a component that contradicts the established impression reads as
inconsistency rather than range.

**4. Narrative is how reviewers hold a file.** The authors' design proposals turn
on narrative and storytelling structure as the way to prune and retain captured
detail — reviewers organise applications as stories.

*Consequence:* a file that resolves into one retellable sentence survives the
compression in (1) and (2). A file that is a list of good things does not.

## How to weigh it

Tier **[P]** — empirical research. Outranks practitioner anecdote because it is
systematic observation of actual reviewer behaviour. It is still one office in
one era, and the authors' purpose was to design better tools, not to describe
admissions policy. Do not extend a finding here into a claim about what any
named college currently does.

The full text is not in this repository. It is available through the ACM Digital
Library under the citation above.
